# Question review

Correctness review of the generated knowledge-point questions (CEN-110), one section per course. The automated checks cover every course and come first.

## Automated checks

`tests/question-quality.test.ts` runs with `npm test` and checks knowledge-point questions for giveaways that the executed-output tests cannot see. The choice checks apply to `choice` questions; the prompt checks (duplicates and missing context) also apply to typed `text` and `numeric` questions:

- **Length tell.** Per course, how often the correct choice is the strictly longest, and how often it is the strictly shortest. Conceptual and output questions are counted separately, because an output's length is fixed by its program. With four choices and lengths unrelated to the key, either tell occurs about 21% of the time here (ties never count). A course fails above 40%: "pick the longest" would then beat chance by more than 15 points, which for a group of 40 or more questions is over three standard deviations, so a fair course does not fail by accident. Limiting the shortest share as well keeps a fix from trimming every key into the opposite tell. Lengths are measured as rendered: `$…$` math counts roughly as KaTeX displays it, and backtick code spans lose their backticks.
- **Far-longer key.** A key of 8 or more characters that is more than 1.8 times longer than every distractor fails on its own.
- **Duplicate choices.** Choices must differ after collapsing whitespace. Plain-prose choices must also differ after ignoring case and punctuation. Code and output keep their spelling, since `self.SCALE`, `"ABC"` and `first second` versus `first\nsecond` are deliberate distinctions.
- **Duplicate prompts.** No two questions in one skill share a prompt and code.
- **Key quoted in the prompt.** A key of two or more words may not appear verbatim in the prompt unless a distractor does too.
- **Context the review does not show.** A review shows one question alone, without the point's explanation, its worked example, or the neighbouring questions (`learning-session.tsx` renders the teaching only in learn mode). Prompts may not refer to "the example", "the class above", a capitalized name "above", or "the previous table/curve/program".

Two candidate checks were left out because they were not reliable. Matching words of the key against the explanation flagged 2,614 of 7,448 questions (35%), and a sample of 30 were all fine: explanations give the reason, not the answer's words. Explanations that point at a choice by position ("only the third pair") are wrong because choices are shuffled, but a heuristic for them also matched references to lines of code; those were found by search and fixed by hand (listed in the course sections and below).

### How the flagged questions were fixed

Compared with current main, 855 questions differ, all by line-local edits: C++ 241, Machine Learning 152, Data Systems 148, Rust 146, Competitive Programming 121, Python for Data Analysis 31, Quantitative foundations 14, Python foundations 2. Edits to questions that CEN-111 later made typed were dropped in the merge, except fixes to their prompts or explanations. No correct-choice index changed, and no executed question's code or correct output changed. The usual fix made the key terser and rewrote one or two flat distractors ("Never", "Nothing", "It is not risky") into specific misconceptions of similar length. Competitive Programming was reviewed separately, so it got distractor edits only, apart from one positional explanation and three keys that were shortened (`cp-fenwick-update-walk-kp1-q4`, `cp-lifting-query-bits-kp2-q3`, `cp-scc-reverse-components-kp2-q4`). Rewrites in the TeX-converted courses keep `$…$` math and `\$` for dollars.

Outside the Rust and C++ review, these edits also fixed:

- `unpacking-kp2-q2` and `cp-stack-pop-kp2-q2`: position-dependent explanations ("the third line", "the second … the last").
- `ml-overfitting-kp1-q4` and `kp2-q2`: prompts that pointed at "the previous table" and "the previous curve" now state the numbers.
- Four distractors written during this work were replaced on review because they were defensibly correct or too close to the key: "A, B, C, D and E" as the nodes reachable from A (A reaches itself by a path of length 0), "Count all users who have an email" as a query an email index cannot help, "301 writes" for 300 embedded copies (the supplier's own record would make 301), and `O((V + E) log V)` for lazy-heap Dijkstra with parallel edges (equal to the key whenever E is polynomial in V).

### Debatable, left alone

- **Loose upper bounds as distractors.** These are `cp-backtracking-kp3-q4` (`O(n^k · k!)`), `cp-heaps-kp3-q2` (`O(n log n + k)`), and `cp-segment-tree-kp3-q4` (`O(n log n)` to build). Each is a true but loose upper bound. They stand in for misconceptions: counting ordered selections, or sorting first. Every point derives the tight bound the question asks for, and the course never teaches the word "tight". If this needs fixing, one convention sentence in the course would cover all three, rather than three separate edits.
- **Expected vs worst case.** `cp-grids-kp3-q1` (`O(RC)` with set lookups) and `cp-sort-copy-kp2-q4` (`sorted` is O(n log n), though Timsort is O(n) on sorted input) both state the bound the lesson teaches, and no distractor is correct. `cp-hashing-kp1-q4` relies on hash collisions, which are not taught, but it can be answered by elimination.
- **`cp-compression-kp3-q1`.** Sorting dominates only when u log u exceeds n. The prompt implies the worst case, u = n.
- **`cp-recursion-kp3-q3`.** "It grows to about e calls" describes the growth rate. The exact count is about 4e. The distractors are O(log e), halving, and log² e.
- **`cp-tries-kp3-q2`.** The choices put constants inside O(). That informal style is used throughout the course, and the intent (cost depends on prefix length, not word count) is clear.
- **`cp-graph-models-kp2-q4`.** It asks about adjacency matrices, but sits in the point on direction and multiplicity. The skill introduction answers it ("A matrix … offers direct edge lookup"), and kp3 repeats the space cost. Moving it would renumber question IDs.
- **`cp-pointer-count-block-kp1-q4`.** The distractor "always have smaller sums" is false when values repeat; the point says "no larger". It is a deliberate strict-inequality distractor.
- **`cp-interval-overlap-kp1-q4`.** With closed intervals (taught in kp2), a second pair would also be misreported. kp1 states half-open intervals.
- **`cp-combinatorics-kp1-q3`.** "modulo the modulus" refers to a modulus defined in the skill introduction, not in kp1.
- **`cp-lis-tail-position-kp1-q3`.** The marked answer gives the purpose of `bisect_left` rather than its mechanism. No distractor is correct.
- **Wording only.** `cp-scc-finish-order-kp2-q1` (list positions read as vertex labels), `cp-combination-pascal-step-kp1-q2` (the fixed item is introduced in the point, not the prompt), `cp-grids-kp1-q2`, `cp-compression-kp1-q4`, `cp-fenwick-kp3-q3`, and `cp-segment-query-boundaries-kp2-q3`.
